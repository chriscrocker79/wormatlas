							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top background="#lamellipodium"><p>This term can refer to  several distinctly different features involving broad flattened surfaces:</p>

Similar broad extensions have also been noted  from differentiating neuron cell bodies in some mutant backgrounds (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00025223;class=Paper" target="_blank">Yang and Lundquist, 2005</a>), at the tips of neuron growth cones (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00001881;class=Paper" target="_blank">Chalfie, 1994</a> -cover photo in Science), from  the somata of the CEP sheath cells to provide a glial wrapping of the nerve  ring, and from the GLR somata as a scaffold for muscle arms of the nerve ring.</p>

										See <a href="gglossary.htm#growthcone" target="_blank">Growth cone</a> <br>

Lack of lamin results in embryonic lethality (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00004416;class=Paper" target="_blank">Liu et al., 2000</a>). </p></td>

							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="larva"></a>Larva/ larval </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"></font>Juvenile (S) <br>
								L1, L2, L3, L4 and dauer stage (S) </td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>In the<a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig6leg.htm" target="_blank"> life cycle</a> of <em>C. elegans</em>, upon hatching from the egg,  the immature nematode progresses through intermediate developmental stages  interrupted by a series of molts until finally reaching adulthood. The &ldquo;<a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig8leg.htm" target="_blank">larval  stages</a>&rdquo; of <em>C. elegans</em> should perhaps be more properly termed &ldquo;nymphal stages&rdquo; since the  animal does not undergo a true metamorphosis, but retains the same basic body  plan and retains virtually all juvenile cells. Normal development progresses from egg to L1, L2, L3, L4 and then  adult. Starved or stressed animals can  enter a <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_dauer/dauer.html" target="_blank">dauer larva stage</a> after L2 and interrupt normal developmental  progression. All larval stages are all  pre-reproductive, lacking mature sexual organs.</p>
									<p><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><br>
										See<a href="dglossary.htm#dauer" target="_blank"> Dauer </a><br>
										See </font><a href="#l2d">L2d</a><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><br>
											See <a href="pglossary.htm#postdauer" target="_blank">Post-dauer </a></font></p></td>
						</tr>

								Lumbar nerve (S)<br>
								Ano-lumbar commissure (S)<br>
								Caudalid (archaic) </p></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top>Each left and right lumbar  commissure connects one of the lumbar ganglia to the pre-anal ganglion via  closely bundled processes passing circumferentially beneath the ventral  bodywall muscles in the tail.
								<p>&nbsp;</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="lumbarganglion"></a><a href="../hermaphroditetailneurons.html" target="_blank">Lumbar ganglion</a> </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><p>&nbsp;</p>
									<font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"></font></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>The <a href="http://www.wormatlas.org/ver1/hall_1991/art/fig1_large.jpg" target="_blank">left and right lumbar ganglia</a> lie behind the pre-anal ganglion in the tail, in  lateral positions. Each contains 12  neuron cell bodies that send most of their <a href="http://www.wormatlas.org/ver1/hall_1991/art/fig5_large.jpg" target="_blank">neuronal processes</a> into the ventral  nerve cord via lumbar commissures and the pre-anal ganglion. There is no local neuropil in these ganglia  in the hermaphrodite, instead they form most synapses in the pre-anal  ganglion. Most lumbar neurons have  sensory functions. In the adult male  tail, these two ganglia gain many additional neurons and have some local  neuropil. The lumbar neurons lie in close association with the lateral  hypodermis, and most are posterior to the anus.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="lumen"></a>Lumen/ lumena </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>The interior of a vessel within a body such as of the <a href="../hermaphrodite/pharynx/Images/phafig3E_Gleg.htm" target="_blank">pharynx</a>, <a href="../hermaphrodite/pharynx/Images/phafig11leg.htm" target="_blank">intestine</a>, <a href="../hermaphrodite/excretory/Images/excfig5leg.htm" target="_blank">excretory gland</a>, <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig11BCleg.htm" target="_blank">uterus</a>, <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig3leg.htm" target="_blank">vulva</a>, or <a href="../hermaphrodite/somatic gonad/Images/somaticfig9leg.htm" target="_blank">spermatheca</a>. It can also refer to the inner membrane space of the <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.2501" target="_blank">mitochondrion</a>, <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.2347" target="_blank">golgi</a> or<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.2205" target="_blank"> ER</a>.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="lysis"></a>Lysis</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"></font></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top>Typically refers to death of a cell by bursting. Can occur by osmotic or viral triggers that compromise the integrity of the cellular membrane. Also refers to the bursting of entire worms for preparation of DNA, RNA or proteins.</td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="lysosome"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.2366" target="_blank">Lysosome</a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A membrane bound organelle that contains digestive enzymes <font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">for digesting macromolecules and <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_autophagy/autophagyfig1leg.jpg" target="_blank">disposing of unwanted materials</a> such as phagocytosed bacterium and damaged organelles.   Additionally, lysosomes aid in the recycling of the components of <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=intracellulartrafficking&rendertype=figure&id=A2630" target="_blank">receptor-mediated endocytosis</a>. Each pathway to degradation leads to the lysosome, regardless of the souce of the material (<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4" target="_blank">Alberts, 2002</a>). </font></p>
								<p><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">See also Wormbook chapters: Intracellular trafficking (<a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_intracellulartrafficking/intracellulartrafficking.html" target="_blank">Grant and Sato, 2006</a>) and  Autophagy in <em>C. elegans</em> (<a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_autophagy/autophagy.html#sec1" target="_blank">Mel&eacute;ndez and Levine, 2009</a>).<br>
									</font></p></td>
						</tr>
