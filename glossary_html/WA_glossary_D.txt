							<td width="262" height="12" valign=top class="style18"><b><a name="dauer" id="dauer"></a><a href="http://www.wormatlas.org/dauer/dauerhomepage.htm" target="_blank">Dauer</a></b></td>
							<td width="150" height="12" valign=top class="style18"><a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_dauer/dauer.html" target="_blank">Dauer 
								larva</a> (S)</td>
							<td width="137" height="12" valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
							<td width="503" height="12" valign=top class="style18"><p>An <a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig6leg.htm" target="_blank">alternative larval stage</a> which <i>C. elegans </i>enters in preference to becoming an L2 larva when 
								placed under conditions of environmental stress, low food supply, or crowding 
								(<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(75)90109-8" target="_blank">Cassada and Russell, 1975</a>; <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2617852/" target="_blank">Riddle, 1978</a>). This stage has <a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig8leg.htm" target="_blank">a longer, thinner 
									body shape</a> than the L2 larva, is less active, and able to cross physical 
								barriers (by ignoring aversive stimuli) to escape its current conditions. 
								The dauer larva has a <a href="../dauer/cuticle/Images/dcutfig4leg.htm?book=ce2&part=A888&rendertype=figure&id=A890" target="_blank">closed mouth</a>  and does not feed. Its intestinal cells are filled with <a href="../dauer/intestine/Images/dintfig3leg.htm" target="_blank">electron dense storage granules</a>. Upon reaching more 
								favorable conditions the dauer molts directly into the L3 stage and resumes 
								normal development. A dauer larva can remain in this stage for four to 
								eight times the normal 3-week life span of nondauer animals. It is covered 
<a href="../dauer/cuticle/Cutframeset.html" target="_blank"> cuticle</a>, and most tissues may be reduced in volume.</p>
									<p>Mutants are available which increase (<a href="#dauerconstitutive">DAuer Formation constitutive</a>; daf-c) 
										or decrease (<a href="#dauerdefective">DAuer Formation defective</a>; daf-d) entry to the dauer state, 
										and some of these genes may be involved in producing longer lifespan.</p>
								<p>Alternatively, the &#147;dauer larva&#148; can also refer to an obligate 
									L3 stage (in some species, an L2 stage) in many parasitic nematode species, 
									which stall their development until they locate the necessary host to 
									support final stages of differentiation (<a href="/handbook/references.htm" target="_blank">Bird 
										and Bird, 1991</a>). Many of these dauers are known to adopt characteristic 
									coiled postures, as well as ceasing to feed.</p>
								<p>See <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_dauer/dauer.html" target="_blank">Hu, 2007</a> in <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/" target="_blank">WormBook</a> for more detail.</p>
								<p> <br>
									See <a href="#diapause">Diapause</a><br>
									See <a href="lglossary.htm#lethargus" target="_blank">Lethargus</a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr>
							<td width="262" height="12" valign=top class="style18"><b><a name="dauerarrest" id="dauerarrest"></a><a href="../dauer/diapause/DNeuroRegframeset.html" target="_blank">Dauer 
								arrest</a></b></td>
							<td width="150" height="12" valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
							<td width="137" height="12" valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
							<td width="503" height="12" valign=top class="style18">Refers 
								to the state of arrested tissue development in the dauer larva. Whereas 
								nematode tissues continuously grow and undergo progressive developmental 
								advances throughout normal larval growth, the dauer larva enters a state 
								of arrest in which no developmental changes occur, until the animal finds 
								more suitable growing conditions, or until it spontaneously shifts back 
								into a normal developmental schedule. This change back to normal growth 
								is often called &#147;exiting dauer&#148; (arrest) or dauer recovery.</td>
						</tr>
						<tr>
							<td width="262" height="12" valign=top class="style18"><b><a name="dauerbehavior"></a><a href="../dauer/behavior/Behaviorframeset.html" target="_blank">Dauer 
								behavior</a></b></td>
							<td width="150" height="12" valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
							<td width="137" height="12" valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
							<td width="503" height="12" valign=top class="style18"><p>Animals that have entered into 
								the dauer state show several general changes in overt behavior. They stop 
								their pharyngeal pumping, there is a modest decline in their rate of body 
								motion, however, they exhibit a complex dispersal behavior. The dispersal 
								behavior motivates the animal to move away from its present surroundings, 
								ignoring aversive stimuli such as osmotic barriers, and to find a more 
								attractive environment in which to resume normal growth. Dauers can often 
								lie motionless and very straight, as if lacking normal muscle tone. However, 
								they remain responsive to touch or chemical stimuli and can then move 
								quickly. </p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="#dispersal">Dispersal</a></p></td>

							<td width="262" height="12" valign=top class="style18"><b><a name="dauercuticle"></a><a href="../dauer/cuticle/Cutframeset.html?rid=ce2.figgrp.890" target="_blank">Dauer

							<td width="503" height="12" valign=top class="style18">The 
								outer cuticle of the dauer larva is specialized to protect the animal during 
								prolonged times of desiccation. The cuticle is much thicker than the normal

								characteristic <a href="../dauer/cuticle/Images/dcutfig2leg.htm" target="_blank">alae along the lateral seam</a> that are different from those

								a remnant portion of outer cuticle which is thought to represent a fragmentary 
								L2 cuticle; this remnant is eventually lost without any change in the status 
								of the dauer animal (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(75)90109-8" target="_blank">Cassada and Russell, 1975</a><a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000058;class=Paper" target="_blank"></a>).</td>

							<td height="12" valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
							<td height="12" valign=top class="style18">The

								which may also serve to aid the animal&#146;s escape from starvation in 
								nature by catching a ride on a passing insect or animal (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(75)90109-8" target="_blank">Cassada and Russell, 1975</a><a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000058;class=Paper" target="_blank"></a>).</td>

							<td width="137" height="12" valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
							<td width="503" height="12" valign=top class="style18">The 
								developmental process through which a stressed L1 larva proceeds to enter 
								into the dauer larva stage in preference to entering the normal L2 stage. 
								A series of tissue changes is involved, including the shrinkage of most 
								tissues to give the animal a darker, much thinner appearance and a higher

							<td width="137" height="12" valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
							<td width="503" height="12" valign=top class="style18">A 
								set of related genes whose sequential expression controls the decision to 
								force the animal to develop into a dauer larva in preference to the normal 
								L2 stage. Mutations in some of these genes can redirect this decision process 
								to force all animals to enter the dauer stage (dauer constitutive) 
								or to prevent all animals from entering dauer (dauer defective), 
								irrespective of the environmental signals.</td>
