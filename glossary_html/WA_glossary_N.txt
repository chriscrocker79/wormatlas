							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><b><a name="necromenic"></a>Necromenic</b></font></span></td>

							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">A common form of cell death  caused by external insults to cellular integrity; in some cases, necrosis may  spread from cell to cell to damage many neighboring cells. Necrotic cells show <a href="http://www.jneurosci.org/cgi/content/full/17/3/1033/F3" target="_blank">early swelling</a> in  cytoplasmic organelles and the nucleus, lytic activity in the cytoplasm, and  late changes in chromatin, while the cell gradually swells in volume before  dying (<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=ce2.section.520" target="_blank">Hengartner, 1997</a>). </p>
									<p class="style35">Necrotic-like cell death in <em>C. elegans</em> has been carefully <a href="http://www.jneurosci.org/cgi/content/full/17/3/1033/F3" target="_blank">characterized in neurons</a> undergoing degenerin-induced cell death (<a href="http://www.jneurosci.org/cgi/content/full/17/3/1033#F3" target="_blank">Hall et al., 1997</a>). </p>
								<p class="style35"><br>
									See <a href="aglossary.htm#apoptosis" target="_blank">Apoptosis</a><br>
									See <a href="pglossary.htm#phagocytosis" target="_blank">Phagocytosis</a></p></td>

							<td valign=top><p class="style35">A characteristic,  specialized pattern of fasciculation among neurites (generated during process  outgrowth) that causes individual neurites to be surrounded by a standard set of  neighbors along a nerve cord or within neuropil; the pattern surrounding any  particular nerve process is called its &ldquo;neighborhood&rdquo; (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000702;class=Paper" target="_blank">White et al., 1983</a>).</p></td>

							<td valign=top><span class="style35">Process bundle (S)<br>
								Nerve cord (S) <br>
								Nerve bundles (S) </span></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">A bundle of thin  neuronal processes running in parallel, connecting different ganglia, sensory  endings, synaptic neuropil, and/or regions of motor output. In the nematode there are generally no glial-like  wrappings (no myelin) associated with a nerve, but virtually all nerves in the  bodywall are closely associated with hypodermis and the hypodermal basal  lamina. Nerves in the pharynx seem to  run independent of any epithelia, often lying within a groove established by  the muscles. Most major nerves in the  nematode run longitudinally and are interconnected via circumferential commissures.</p>
									<p class="style35"><strong>Named nerves:<br>
										</strong>Amphid (2) = Chemosensory<br>
										Dorsal cord <br>
										Labial (6) = Mechanosensory <br>
										Dorsal (pharynx)<br>
										Lateral (2)<br>
										Lumbar-preanal (2)  [runs as a pair of commissures] <br>
										Pharyngeal (3) =  Dorsal and Subventral (2) <br>
										Phasmid (2) <br>
										Ventral Cord (2) </p>

										See <a href="cglossary.htm#cord" target="_blank">Cord/ Chord</a> <br>

Can also refer to a neuronal process running  independently away from an organized nerve cord.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><a name="nervering"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/nervous/Images/neurofig1leg.htm" target="_blank">Nerve ring</a></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">The major zone of overlap,  among approximately 200 overlapping axons in the head, which<a href="../hermaphrodite/nervous/Images/neurofig19leg.htm" target="_blank"> encircles the isthmus of the  pharynx</a> to form a <a href="../hermaphrodite/muscleintro/Images/musfig5Gleg.htm" target="_blank">tightly packed ring-like structure</a> on the outside of the  pharynx. The nerve ring lies directly  over a cylindrical muscle plate formed by arms from the head muscles. The nerve ring is the most concentrated zone  of synaptic interconnections among the neurons of the head, and most head  neurons lie very close by, such that the nerve ring and its associated neurons  could be considered one huge ganglion, acting as &ldquo;the brain&rdquo; of the nematode. </p>
									<p class="style35">A much <a href="../hermaphrodite/pharynx/Images/phafig9A_Eleg.htm" target="_blank">smaller ring of axons lies within the  pharynx</a>, formed by neurites extended from many pharyngeal neurons. Head neurons form almost no synapses away  from the nerve ring or the longitudinal motor nerves.</p>
								<p class="style35"><br>
									See <a href="pglossary.htm#pharyngealnervering" target="_blank">Pharyngeal nerve ring</a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><b><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="nervoussystem"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/nervous/Neuroframeset.html" target="_blank">Nervous system</a> </font></b></span></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35">Neurons (S)<br>
								Nervous tissue (S) </span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">The nervous system of<em> C. elegans</em> is formed from 302 neurons, each of which has unique properties, morphologies and connectivities. While <em>C. elegans</em> does not have a &quot;brain&quot; per se, most of the nervous system is situated in the head region which is where most of the sensing by the animal occurs. For more details see <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_specnervsys/specnervsys.html" target="_blank">Hobert, 2005</a>. </p>
									<p class="style35"><br>
										See <a href="#nervering">Nerve ring</a> </p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="neuralplasticity"></a>Neural plasticity </font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">Functional changes in the nervous system ranging from changes at the synapses during development (<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=ce2.section.916" target="_blank">Jorgensen and Rankin, 1997</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_specnervsys/specnervsys.html#d0e1561" target="_blank">Hobert, 2005</a>) to changes in behavior of the adult animal (<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=ce2.section.924" target="_blank">Jorgensen and Rankin, 1997</a>). Behavioral assays have been developed to test for neural plasticity by chemotaxis (<a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_behavior/behavior.html#sec4_1" target="_blank">Jensen, 2005</a> and <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_behavior/behavior.html#sec5_6" target="_blank">Mori, 2005</a> in <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_behavior/behavior.html" target="_blank">Hart, 2006</a>).</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="neurite"></a>Neurite</font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">Axon or dendrite (S)<br>
								Process or branch or Commissure (S) <br>
								Nerve fiber (S) <br>
							</font></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">This term is often used  preferentially to &ldquo;dendrite&rdquo; or  &ldquo;axon&rdquo; to refer to neuronal processes with mixed functions (e.g. having some  sensory role and also an important role in synaptic interactions), or to an unspecialized neuronal process which  has not yet developed a specific function.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="neuroblast"></a>Neuroblast</font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">Blast cell (S) <br>
								Epidermoblast (S) </font></span></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">Precursor, or stem cell, to  neurons. In <em>C. elegans </em>there are few  true neuroblasts since most neuronal lineages are not pure; their precursors  yield mixtures of neurons, structural cells and hypodermis.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="neurocraticcontroloflocomotion" id="neurocraticcontroloflocomotion"></a>Neurocratic control of locomotion</font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">A theory of behavioral  control of nematode locomotion in which the primary role of the nervous system  is to switch between alternate waveforms, where each waveform is chiefly  generated by a &ldquo;myogenic&rdquo; control mechanism (<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/query.fcgi?db=pubmed&cmd=Retrieve&dopt=AbstractPlus&list_uids=5139022&query_hl=3&itool=pubmed_docsum" target="_blank">Crofton, 1971</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000076;class=Paper" target="_blank">Croll, 1975</a>),  inherent to muscle-muscle interconnections.</p>
									<p class="style35"><br>
										See <a href="mglossary.htm#myogeniccontroloflocomotion" target="_blank">Myogenic control of locomotion</a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="neurofilament"></a>Neurofilament</font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">Intermediate filament (S) </p></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">Any type of filamentous protein lying within the  cytoplasm, e.g. any filamentous cytoskeletal component within a neuron soma or  process, especially those whose biochemical nature is not immediately  known. However, the term is most often  used to refer to an intermediate filament.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><b><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="neurogenesis"></a>Neurogenesis</font></b></span></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>

							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="nuclearmigration"></a>Nuclear migration </font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p><span class="style30"><span class="style33"><span class="style35"><span class="style35"></span></span></span></span></p></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">Certain cell nuclei make characteristic movements during embryogenesis  (at comma stage) or later during tissue morphogenesis. During <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_epidermalmorphogenesis/epidermalmorphogenesis.html#d0e769" target="_blank">embryonic closure</a>, some hypodermal  nuclei move within extended processes of the hypodermal cells to migrate from  one side of the body to the other, crossing the dorsal midline. Similarly, in mid L1 stage, certain precursor  nuclei (Pn cells) migrate from lateral positions to the ventral midline prior  to dividing to produce neurons of the ventral nerve cord. It is most likely that nuclei may be actively  moved via the internal cytoskeleton by motor proteins, or perhaps pushed  passively by the squeezing actions of adjacent cells. Some mutants are known to block the normal  progress of these migrations, including the lineage mutants unc-83,unc-84, and  lin-3 (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000500;class=Paper" target="_blank">Sulston and Horvitz, 1981</a>).</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="nuclearpore"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.2157" target="_blank">Nuclear pore</a> </font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">Periodic, highly organized,  channels (patent holes) in the nuclear membrane across which macromolecules  including mRNAs and transcription factors can be transported between the  nucleus and the cytoplasm. They are evenly  spaced throughout the membrane and are most easily seen by freeze fracture.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="nucleolus"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.1044"nucleolus"+AND+mboc4[book]+AND+372690[uid]&rid=mboc4.figgrp.1044" target="_blank">Nucleolus</a></font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p><span class="style30"><span class="style33"><span class="style35"><span class="style35"></span></span></span></span></p></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">A darkly staining part of the nucleus that is the site for <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.1047" target="_blank">the processing of rRNAs and their assembly into ribosomes</a>. </p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="nucleosome"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.595" target="_blank">Nucleosome</a></font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p><span class="style30"><span class="style33"><span class="style35"><span class="style35"></span></span></span></span></p></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">The basic level of chromatin packing in which the DNA is wrapped around a protein core formed from histones. This gives a &quot;<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.631" target="_blank">beads on a string</a>&quot; look to the DNA. Each nucleosome core particle is separated from the next by a region of<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.632" target="_blank"> linker DNA</a>. Nucleosomes are then usually packed together into compact stacks generating a condensed <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.641" target="_blank">30 nm fiber</a>. </p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="nucleus"></a>Nucleus</font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">Membrane enclosed organelle which contains most of the cell's genetic material. </p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="nulloxsperm"></a>Nullo-X sperm </font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">A sperm cell that contains  no copy of the normal X chromosome. Males produce equal numbers of haplo-X and nullo-X sperm so that half their progeny will also be male. </p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="nursecells"></a>Nurse cells </font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">Germ cells in the somatic  gonad are produced in excess numbers in comparison to the final yield of mature  spermatocytes and oocytes, such that many individual germ cell nuclei undergo  programmed cell death as they approach the loop of the gonad arm. Since all germ cells prior to this locale form  a single syncytium, connected by a central rachis, it is postulated that the  excess germ cells and especially their nuclei serve as nurse cells to those  germ cells which do survive (<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?call=bv.View..ShowSection&rid=ce2.chapter.485" target="_blank">Hengartner, 1997</a>), synthesizing components which  are delivered into the rachis to feed the surviving cells prior to their  cellularization.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="nymph"></a>Nymph</font></strong></span></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style35"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p class="style35">See <a href="lglossary.htm#larva" target="_blank">Larva </a></p></td>
						</tr>
