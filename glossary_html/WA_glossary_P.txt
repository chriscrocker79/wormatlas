							<td valign=top><p>Sensory endings  that create small, visible lumps in the cuticle surrounding the dendritic  specialization. Examples include the <a href="../hermaphrodite/neuronalsupport/Images/neurofig37leg.htm" target="_blank">deirids</a>, <a href="../male/postcloacal/images/MalePCSFIG3A.jpg" target="_blank">post-cloacal sensillae (PCS)</a>, <a href="../male/hook/images/MaleHookFIG1.jpg" target="_blank">hook</a>, and<a href="../hermaphrodite/neuronalsupport/Images/neurofig34leg.htm" target="_blank"> inner labial sensillae of the lips</a>. A  longer, bristle-like sensillium would alternately be termed a &quot;seta&quot;. Mechanoreceptors might form  subtle cuticular features on the lips, but we do not see papillae by SEM  for these structures; instead they apparently form nubbins within the cuticle  that produce no visible specialization when viewed from the surface. </p>
									<p>During morphogenesis, more complex sensilla  (<a href="../male/rays/images/MaleRayFIG1.jpg" target="_blank">male rays</a>, for example) may also adopt the form of papillae prior to their  final elongation phase, which in some developmental mutants may remain as papillae. Some papilla may form a closed structure (as in  the case of the <a href="../hermaphrodite/neuronalsupport/jump.html?newLink=mainframe.htm&newAnchor=Anteriorandposteriordeiridsensilla35" target="_blank">deirid</a>) or they could be open to the exterior environment (as in the<a href="../hermaphrodite/neuronalsupport/jump.html?newLink=mainframe.htm&newAnchor=Innerlabialsensilla33" target="_blank"> inner labial sensilla</a>). Alternately, the whole structure of any of the <a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig4Aleg.htm" target="_blank">six lips</a> may be considered a papilla, containing several neighboring sensilla per  papilla (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000205;class=Paper" target="_blank">Klass et al., 1976</a>).</p>

							<td valign=top><em>Par</em>titioning defective (S) </td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>An embryonic defect (<a href="http://www.mbg.cornell.edu/cals/mbg/research/kemphues-lab/par-mutations.cfm" target="_blank">par mutation</a>) in which cell polarity of the single cell  embryo is disrupted, and cytoplasmic organelles (<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26861/figure/A3828/" target="_blank">P granules</a>, <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=celldivision&rendertype=figure&id=A2131" target="_blank">chromosomes</a>) and  molecular species (mRNAs, protein kinases, and others) fail to segregate  (partition) into specific regions of the cell in normal fashion, disrupting  cell division and normal cell fate decisions. Correct <a href="http://www.mbg.cornell.edu/cals/mbg/research/kemphues-lab/par-proteins.cfm" target="_blank">localization of the PAR proteins</a> facilitates proper cell polarity. (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00004134;class=Paper" target="_blank">Kemphues, 2000</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_asymcelldiv/asymcelldiv.html#d0e451" target="_blank">Gonczy and Rose, 2005</a>). </p></td>

							<td valign=top><b><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="paracrytallinearray"></a>Paracrystal/ Paracrystalline array </font></b></td>
							<td valign=top><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"></font></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>Large molecules may  associate with one another in highly ordered forms to create higher order  complexes, known as &ldquo;<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK28209/figure/A24/" target="_blank">paracrystalline arrays</a>&rdquo;. These arrays may be seen by light  microscopy under polarized light as &ldquo;birefringence&rdquo;. Such features are a normal aspect some cells  such as muscles. Paracrystals can also  form when mutated or poorly processed molecules associate into abnormal aggregates, and these may  also be scored under polarized light (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000399;class=Paper" target="_blank">Waterston et al., 1977</a>).</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="bglossary.htm#birefringence" target="_blank">Birefringence</a></p></td>

						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="paralysis"></a>Paralysis</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><br>
							</font></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>A loss of muscle function that usually comes from damage to the nervous system, but can also come from damage to the muscles. It can be a feature of specific genetic mutations that cause paralysis directly, such as in the case of some <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/phenotype?name=unc" target="_blank">unc</a> (uncoordinated) mutants, or can result from external causes such as exposure to anoxia and high temperatures. Certain <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_acetylcholine/cholinefig1.jpg" target="_blank">mutations</a> and chemicals (<a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_acetylcholine/cholinefig2.jpg" target="_blank">such as levamisole and aldicarb</a>) that promote acetylcholine buildup at the synapses cause a hypercontracted paralysis (<a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_acetylcholine/acetylcholine.html" target="_blank">Rand, 2006</a>). Infection by pathogens also leads to a paralysis which is often followed by death (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00003860;class=Paper" target="_blank">Darby et al., 1999</a>). </p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="paramyosin"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=ce2.figgrp.547" target="_blank">Paramyosin</a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>This protein, which is only found in invertebrate muscle, serves as a  template for <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=ce2.figgrp.533" target="_blank">thick filament assembly in bodywall muscles in <em>C. elegans</em></a>. Myosin heavy chain proteins bind to paramyosin  to form an elongated rod-like multimeric structure, the thick filament.</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="tglossary.htm#thickfilament" target="_blank">Thick filament</a></p></td>
						</tr>

							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>Nematode species often live  as parasites, feeding on or inside of another &ldquo;host&rdquo; species, which may be a  plant, animal, or even another annelid.&nbsp; </p>
									<p><strong>Obligate parasites </strong>cannot exist and reproduce apart from their host. </p>
								<p><strong>Facultative  parasites</strong> may survive separately from their normal host species, but occasionally infect a host organism.&nbsp; </p>
								<p><strong>Pathogenic parasites</strong> cause the death of  their host.&nbsp; </p>
								<p><strong>Ectoparasites</strong> live on the outer surface their host.&nbsp; </p>
								<p><strong>Endoparasites</strong> live inside the body of their host.</p>
								<p> Parasites  typically depend upon their hosts for more than just a supply of food. They may also receive enzymes and  developmental signals that guide and control their further maturation, and help  in dispersal (transport to a new host). </p>
								<p><br>

							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pheromone"></a>Pheromone</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>Any substance (usually an  odorant) released from the body that may act as a signal (attractant or  repellant) to other members of the same species, either as a sexual attractant  or as a social signal (aggregant or repellant).</p>
									<p> Sexual pheromones are known from many nematode species (Bird and  Bird, 1991). A  sexual pheromone (of unknown chemistry) is likely to underlie the distinctive  &ldquo;leaving&rdquo; behavior observed in <em>C. elegans</em> males (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00005617;class=Paper" target="_blank">Lipton and Emmons, 2003</a>). </p>
								<p>A &ldquo;<a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_dauer/dauer.html#sec5" target="_blank">dauer pheromone</a>&rdquo;  induces larval <em>C. elegans </em>to alter their developmental status to enter the <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_dauer/dauer.html#sec6" target="_blank">dauer pathway</a> when animals  are overcrowded (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000503;class=Paper" target="_blank">Albert et al., 1981</a>). This pheromone, called <a href="http://www.kdr.co.kr/Daumone/main.asp" target="_blank">daumone</a>, has been purified and synthesized 6-(3,5-dihydroxy-6-methyltetrahydropyran-2-yloxy)heptanoic                    acid (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00024930;class=Paper" target="_blank">Jeong et al., 2005</a>) and is secreted and sensed by all animals  to determine nematode density. </p>
								<p>An aggregating pheromone is sensed by the <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/ASHframeset.html" target="_blank">ASH</a> or <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/ADLframeset.html" target="_blank">ADL</a> nociceptive neurons of the amphids, and can induce social feeding  behavior in <em>C. elegans</em> (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00005511;class=Paper" target="_blank">DeBono et al., 2002</a>).  Curiously, this unknown chemical signal is aversive, yet stimulates the  animals to group together. This social feeding response is antagonized by a  parallel signaling event transduced by reception of a secreted signal within  the pseudocoelom by other sense cells (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00005512;class=Paper" target="_blank">Coates and DeBono, 2002</a>). </p>
								<p>Secreted  signals that cause signaling between different species of animals are called  allelochemics (Bird and Bird, 1991). The  physical source(s) of pheromone secretion is unknown, but would seem likely to  overlap with the postulated sources of the glycocalyx. It is possible that several of the above  responses involve the same pheromone, perhaps acting on the same or different  receptors at different stage in the animal&rsquo;s development.</p>

							<td valign=top>Apoptosis (S) </td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A common form of cell death which appears to originate as a decision within the nucleus to initiate a cascade of cellular events inside both the nucleoplasm and the cytoplasm resulting in chromatin clumping, nuclear breakdown, cell shrinkage and eventual death. 							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>Sense of one's own body that leads to the orientation and coordination of movement. In <em>C. elegans</em>, proper proprioception generates <a href="http://labs.bio.unc.edu/Goldstein/crawl.mov" target="_blank">a sinusoidal pattern of movement</a>. (1975) </a>suggested that  some receptors of the lips could be proprioceptive by analogy to the structure  of campaniform sensilla in insects, which are believed to detect physical  stressing of the integument. It is also  possible that some receptors may be responsive to both mechanical sensations  produced by objects encountered in the local environment and from stretching  of the cuticle, spicules, or buccal cavity during self-produced body motions.</p></td>

							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A category of epithelial  tissue within the uterus, involving those portions <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig2leg.htm" target="_blank">proximal to the vulva </a>(e.g.  the dorsal epithelium, du, and the ventral epithelium, uv2 and uv3) (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00002608;class=Paper" target="_blank">Newman  et al., 1996</a>).</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudocleavage"></a><a href="http://www.bio.unc.edu/faculty/goldstein/lab/flows.mov" target="_blank">Pseudocleavage</a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A distinct period preceding  the complete division of the one-cell embryo during which directed flow of  cortical cell components cause rearrangements which prefigure an asymmetry to  the first cleavage (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00001741;class=Paper" target="_blank">Hird and White, 1993</a>).  These changes include polarized flow of cytoplasm, pronuclear migration,  and reorganization of cortical actin networks just under the plasma membrane,  perhaps organized by the orientation of the mitotic spindle and a contractile  ring during cytokinesis.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudocleavagefurrow"></a><a href="http://www.bio.unc.edu/faculty/goldstein/lab/flows.mov" target="_blank">Pseudocleavage furrow </a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>Pseudocleavage constriction (S) </td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A belt-like region of indentation causing a noticeable narrowing or  constriction of the one-cell embryo along the midline, about halfway between  the anterior and posterior poles which closely resembles the cleavage furrow  that would occur after a nuclear mitosis as a step in cell division. While this furrow disappears after fusion of the pronuclei, a few minutes later the new diploid nucleus does undergo  mitosis and a new cleavage furrow is generated in virtually the same place. The cell then divides the two subsequent  cell daughters (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000667;class=Paper" target="_blank">Albertson, 1984</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00001741;class=Paper" target="_blank">Hird and White, 1993</a>).</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudocoelom"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/pericellular/jump.html?newLink=mainframe.htm&newAnchor=Pseudocoelom2" target="_blank">Pseudocoelom</a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>Pseudocoelomic cavity (S) <br>
								Body cavity (S)<br>
								Pseudocoel (S) <br>
								Peri-enteric fluid (S) </td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A<a href="../hermaphrodite/pericellular/Images/perifig2leg.htm" target="_blank"> fluid-filled space</a> which separates the bodywall from internal organs. <a href="../hermaphrodite/pericellular/Images/perifig1leg.htm" target="_blank">Within this space</a> the  digestive tract and reproductive tract lie separately, each enclosed by its own  basal lamina. All tissues have their basal side turned towards this cavity and are covered there by basement membranes. Intercellular signals,  nutrients and waste products can travel between tissues within this space. Pseudocoelom fluids are prevented from leakage by barrier junctions in the surrounding epithelium.</p>
									<p> <br>
										See <a href="bglossary.htm#blastocoel" target="_blank">Blastocoel</a><br>
										See <a href="cglossary.htm#coelomiccavity" target="_blank">Coelomic cavity</a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudocoelomicfluid"></a>Pseudocoelomic fluid </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>The contents of the cavity;  it may contain hypertonic salt solutions to maintain the animal&rsquo;s turgor  pressure, and to provide a medium through which signals and waste products can  flow.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudocoelomate"></a>Pseudocoelomate</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>Aschelminthes (S) </td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A classification of  primitive animals characterized by the presence of the pseudocoelom. This  includes roundworms, rotifers, gastrotrichs, and others.&nbsp;</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudocoelomicmembrane"></a>Pseudocoelomic membrane </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A spongy structure bounding  the pseudocoelomic cavity.<a href="../hermaphrodite/pericellular/Images/perifig2leg.htm" target="_blank"> In <em>C. elegans </em>it consists  of basal laminae of the  major tissues</a>, where as in other nematode species it may contain some cell processes, fibrils, and tubular  systems (Bird and Bird, 1991).</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudocoelomocyte"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/coelomocyte/Coelomoframeset.html" target="_blank">Pseudocoelomocyte</a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>See <a href="cglossary.htm#coelomocyte" target="_blank">Coelomocyte </a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudogamy"></a>Pseudogamy/ Pseudogamous</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A form of parthenogenesis  in which sperm is necessary to activate egg development, but where the male chromosomes are discarded, with the sperm pronucleus degenerating without fusing to the female  pronucleus. This condition can occur in  species that are hermaphroditic or bisexual (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000255;class=Paper" target="_blank">Nicholas, 1975</a>; <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/query.fcgi?db=pubmed&cmd=Retrieve&dopt=AbstractPlus&list_uids=769504&query_hl=3&itool=pubmed_docsum" target="_blank">Anya, 1976</a>).  It is not known to occur in <em>C. elegans</em>, but has been reported for <em>Rhabditis aberrans</em>, another hermaphroditic species (Kruger, 1913).</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudomale"></a>Pseudomale</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A mutant intersex phenotype  in which an animal exhibits some or all of the male tissues (male gonad, male  copulatory structures) while carrying the XX chromosomes. Some   fail to copulate successfully with hermaphrodites or females due to lack of  normal mating behavior (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000205;class=Paper" target="_blank">Klass et al., 1976</a>), but others are capable  of producing cross-progeny (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000178;class=Paper" target="_blank">Hodgkin and Brenner, 1977</a>), or may be fertile  hermaphrodites despite having secondary male features (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000478;class=Paper" target="_blank">Herman and Horvitz,  1980</a>).</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="iglossary.htm#intersex" target="_blank">Intersex</a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudopod"></a>Pseudopodium/ Pseudopod/ Pseudopodia </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>An elongated cell extension,  more narrow than a lamellipodium, but broader than a filopodium, which may be  extended in advance of a migrating cell or cell process. They are  present as <a href="http://www.mcb.arizona.edu/wardlab/spermtem.html" target="_blank">leading extensions on spermatozoa</a>, developing neuronal processes,  migrating hypodermal processes and somatic sheath cells. Migrating cells displaying trailing  pseudopodia include the distal tip cell of the gonad.</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="fglossary.htm#filopodium" target="_blank">Filopodium/ Filopodia</a><br>
										See <a href="gglossary.htm#growthcone" target="_blank">Growth cone</a><br>
										See <a href="lglossary.htm#lamellipodium" target="_blank">Lamellipodium/ Lamellipod/ Lamellipodia</a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudopodelongation" id="pseudopodelongation"></a>Pseudopod elongation </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A required component of  pseudopod motility, apparently stemming from <a href="http://www.mcb.arizona.edu/wardlab/treadingvid.html" target="_blank">membrane flow within the pseudopod</a> (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000529;class=Paper" target="_blank">Ward et al., 1981</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00002791;class=Paper" target="_blank">Achanzar and Ward, 1997</a>).&nbsp;The <a href="http://www.mcb.arizona.edu/wardlab/crawlingvid.html" target="_blank">spermatozoon moves by amoeboid action</a> of the pseudopod, extending  membrane ruffles and short cell extensions to guide the cell forward, somewhat  like the movement of a neuron growth cone.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudopodinitiation"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/germ line/Images/germfig9leg.htm" target="_blank">Pseudopod initiation</a> </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>An early step in the  creation of the pseudopod in a maturing spermatozoon, apparently due to the  fusion of membranous organelles with the plasma membrane to contribute membrane  and fibrous material to the growing pseudopod (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000529;class=Paper" target="_blank">Ward et al., 1981</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00002791;class=Paper" target="_blank">Achanzar and  Ward, 1997</a>).</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pseudovulva"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=cellfusion&rendertype=figure&id=A2333" target="_blank">Pseudovulva</a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>An aberrant multicellular  structure caused by excessive inductive signals acting upon the VPC blast cells  to create an extra (nonfunctional) vulval specialization by inducing extra cell  divisions to create extra pi  blast cells and their progeny. These can  generally been seen by light microscopy as one or more small protrusions along  the ventral surface of the adult animal, flanking the location of the true  vulva.</p>
									<p> <br>
										See <a href="mglossary.htm#multivulvaphenotype" target="_blank">Multivulva phenotype </a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="pumping"></a><a href="http://www.mcb.arizona.edu/wardlab/eatingvid.html" target="_blank">Pumping</a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>See <a href="#pharyngealpumping">Pharyngeal pumping </a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="punctate"></a>Punctate</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>Staining patterns seen by light microscopy in which the stain  highlights discrete spots (puncta) as opposed to diffuse staining. This term is often used with respect to  staining for synaptic proteins, in which <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=eurekah&part=A58943&rendertype=figure&id=A58951" target="_blank">labeling for either pre-synaptic  proteins</a> or <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=ionotropicglutatmaterecep&rendertype=figure&id=A6485" target="_blank">post-synaptic proteins tends to be punctate</a>, but also can refer to repeating patterns such as those in seen <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=sarcomereassemblycelegansmusc&rendertype=figure&id=A2685" target="_blank">muscle cells</a>. The &ldquo;punctate&rdquo; object should be compact but  may be irregular in shape.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="puncta"></a>Punctum/ Puncta </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>Dot (S) </td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>Individual structure(s) that  have small compact shapes, such as <a href="../hermaphrodite/nervous/Images/neurofig2DEleg.htm?rid=mechano.figgrp.193" target="_blank">synaptic swellings along an axon</a>. The term  implies the cohesive compact shape less than the exact size, since the punctate  shape of one signal may overlap that of another punctate signal of different  size, referring to separate components of a single larger element (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00005543;class=Paperhttp://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00005543;class=Paper" target="_blank">Hallam  et al., 2002</a>). In a mutant background,  the term is sometimes still used to refer to objects that are more compact in  wild type, but have become distorted and diffuse due to mutation.</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="sglossary.htm#synapticterminal" target="_blank">Synaptic terminal</a></p></td>
						</tr>
