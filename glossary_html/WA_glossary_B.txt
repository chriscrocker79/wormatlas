								<td valign=top class="style18"><p class="style58">&quot;Backing&quot; describes 
									several spontaneous and induced behaviors of <i>C. elegans</i>:</p>
										<p class="style58"><strong>1)</strong> Backward locomotion; backing 
											behavior: Spontaneous backward crawl. Forward locomotion and backward 
											locomotion are antagonistic behaviors, controlled by distinct neural circuits, 
											although these circuits functionally interact. The neuronal circuit comprising <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/AVAframeset.html" target="_blank">AVA</a>, <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/AVDframeset.html" target="_blank">AVD</a> and the A motor neurons drives backward movement. </p>
									<p class="style58"><strong>2)</strong> Backing behavior: <a href="../male/muscleintro/jump.html?newLink=mainframe.htm&newAnchor=MaleMusFIG2" target="_blank">Male specific 
										behavior</a> (see <a href="../movies/Mating.mov" target="_blank">mating behavior movie</a>) where after the male's tail comes into contact with the hermaphrodite, the male backs sharply, curving his tail until his ventral side becomes apposed to the hermaphrodite. He then continues backing, sliding along the hermaphrodite's body and turning sharply near its ends (male avoids the head and tail of the hermaphrodite by turning either under or over the hermaphrodite body near but before it reaches the ends) until his tail 
										reaches the vulva (<a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_malematingbehavior/malematingbehavior.html" target="_blank">Barr and Garcia, 2006</a>). The <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/PVYframeset.html" target="_blank">PVY </a>interneuron is required for continued 
										backing.</p>
									<p class="style58"><strong>3)</strong> Backing response; backing 
										avoidance response; backing reflex: Worms that bump into obstacles (e.g. 
										glass beads) on their paths or that are touched with a fine hair or that 
										encounter high osmotic strength crawl backwards typically for several 
											body lengths. After this, they usually turn around. This response is mediated by the <a href="../hermaphrodite/nervous/Images/neurofig9A-Cleg.htm" target="_blank">mechanosensory touch neurons</a>. </p></td>
