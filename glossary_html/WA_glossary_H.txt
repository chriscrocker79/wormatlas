							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>The reduction or absence of  response after continued stimulation.&nbsp;This behavioral plasticity is commonly observed in <em>C. elegans</em> after  repeated tapping of the head or tail with a hair or eyelash (See <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_behavior/behavior.html#sec2_6" target="_blank">Rankin, 2005</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_mechanosensation/mechanosensation.html#d0e229" target="_blank">Goodman, 2006</a>). 							<td valign=top><p><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"></font>Hard touch (S)<br>
								Gentle touch (A) </p></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>An aversive mechanical stimulus (e.g. poking the body with a  metal wire), which is different than response to &ldquo;gentle touch&rdquo; (for instance  with an eyelash to the bodywall).&nbsp; Harsh  touch is transduced by the <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/PVDframeset.html" target="_blank">PVD</a> sensilla in the back half of the body (<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/query.fcgi?cmd=Search&db=books&doptcmdl=GenBookHL&term=%22harsh+touch%22+AND+ce2%5Bbook%5D+AND+1704%5Buid%5D&rid=ce2.section.785#796" target="_blank">Driscoll and Kaplan, 1997</a>). Study of the genetics of sensory mechanotransduction have helped to elucidate the cellular and molecular components required to respond to harsh (and gentle) touch (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00005666;class=Paper" target="_blank">Ernstrom and Chalfie, 2002</a>). </p>

							<td valign=top><span class="style25"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A multi-step process, first described for <em>C. elegans</em> by <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000354;class=Paper" target="_blank">Singh and Sulston (1978)</a>, by  which the developing embryo makes an opening in the egg shell and exits  this covering. Secretions from pharyngeal glands may promote  he gradual weakening of the eggshell, which becomes visibly softer and more  pliable in the hour just before eclosion. Once weakened, the motions of the <a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig7leg.htm" target="_blank">three-fold embryo</a> cause  distortions of the whole shell and eventually the eggshell ruptures, allowing the new L1 larva to emerge from the broken  shell.</p>

						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="hemiadherensjunction"></a>Hemi-adherens junction</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"></font>Hemidesmosome (S) <br>
								Dense body (S)<br>
								Half desmosome (S) </td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>A form of cellular attachment to  extracellular matrices, homologous to the adherens junction, in which an  asymmetric junction forms between a plasma membrane and a neighboring  extracellular matrix (either basal lamina or cuticle). When attachments are to cuticle, there is  often a corresponding dense specialization within the inner cuticle layer  linked to the density on the plasma membrane. These junctions are common where hypodermis faces cuticle, and on the  basal surface of some other epithelia, such as gonad sheath. The  muscle dense body is probably a highly specialized form of hemi-adherens junction. </p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="aglossary.htm#adherensjunction" target="_blank">Adherens junction</a><br>
										See <a href="dglossary.htm#densebody" target="_blank">Dense body</a><br>
										See <a href="fglossary.htm#fibrousorganelle" target="_blank">Fibrous organelle </a></p></td>
						</tr>

									<p><br>
										See <a href="aglossary.htm#amphidcommissure" target="_blank">Amphid commissure</a> <br>
										See <a href="cglossary.htm#caudalids" target="_blank">Caudalids</a> <br>

						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="hermaphrodite"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig1leg.htm" target="_blank">Hermaphrodite</a>/ Hermaphroditism </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><br>
							</font></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>The more common sexual form of the  nematode, <em>Caenorhabditis elegans</em>, in which the gonad generates both male and  female gametes. The hermaphrodite is  self-fertile, since its gametes can be brought together internally to create  healthy progeny. These animals can also  participate in <a href="../movies/Mating.mov" target="_blank">sexual unions</a> with <a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig5leg.htm" target="_blank">male animals</a> to create cross-progeny by  internal fertilization of the hermaphrodite&rsquo;s female gamete, or oocyte, with  the male&rsquo;s sperm.<br>
											<br>
											<br>
								See <a href="aglossary.htm#automictic" target="_blank">Automictic</a><br>
								See <a href="gglossary.htm#gonochoristic" target="_blank">Gonochoristic</a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="herniation"></a>Herniation</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"></font></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>A discrete bulging or local rupture in the  basal lamina which allows a cell process to cross to the &ldquo;wrong&rdquo; side of a  tissue border. </p>
									<p>The  same term might be used to describe a discrete bulging in any other thin  membrane or tissue.</p>
								<p><br>
									See <a href="bglossary.htm#basallamina" target="_blank">Basal lamina</a><br>
									See <a href="fglossary.htm#fenestration" target="_blank">Fenestration</a></p></td>
