                    but are otherwise extremely similar in behavior and ecology; they are likely 
                    to be very close relatives evolutionarily (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000277;class=Paper" target="_blank">Nigon

                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style34"></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33">A species 
                    of nematode, or roundworm, that has been adopted as a model organism for 
                    the study of genetics, cell biology, development, and neuroscience. Two 
                    different wild strains, Bristol and Bergerac, were initially adopted by <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000277;class=Paper" target="_blank">Nigon 
                      and Dougherty (1949)</a> and by <a href="http://elegans.swmed.edu/wli/%5Bcgc31%5D/?%5Cbbr%5Be%E9%E8%EA%EB%5D%5Bn%F1%5D%5Bn%F1%5D%5Be%E9%E8%EA%EB%5Dr%5Cb%3B%5Cb%5Be%E9%E8%EA%EB%5Dl%5Be%E9%E8%EA%EB%5Dg%5Ba%AA%E1%E0%E2%E5%E4%E3%E6%5D%5Bn%F1%5D%5Bs%DF%5D%5Cb" target="_blank">Brenner 
                        (1974)</a> as the basis for scientific studies, although additional wild-caught 
                    strains have since been collected around the world for comparisons of genetic 
                    diversity. The sequence of the<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genome/41" target="_blank"> entire genome</a> was published in 1998 (with the last gaps finished in 2002) making <em>C. elegans</em> the first multi-cellular eukarote in which <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov//blast/Blast.cgi?PAGE_TYPE=BlastSearch&PROG_DEF=blastn&BLAST_PROG_DEF=megaBlast&BLAST_SPEC=OGP__6239__9548" target="_blank">every base is known</a> (<em><a href="http://genome.wustl.edu/publications/detail/genome-sequence-of-the-nematode-c-elegans-a-platform-for-investigating-biology-the-c-elegans-sequencing-consortium/" target="_blank">C. elegans </a></em><a href="http://genome.wustl.edu/publications/detail/genome-sequence-of-the-nematode-c-elegans-a-platform-for-investigating-biology-the-c-elegans-sequencing-consortium/" target="_blank">Sequencing Consortium</a><a href="http://www.sciencemag.org/content/282/5396/2012.abstract">, 1998</a>).</span></td>
                </tr>

Additionally, it appears that unlike <em>C. elegans, C. japonica</em> may enter the dauer stage regardless of food and environmental conditions. This species is usually found in close association with stink bugs. <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genome/252" target="_blank">Full sequencing of the genome</a> is currently in the initial stages. </span></td>

                          1997</a>), as is the cuticle of very old adults (<a href="../images/Herndon 2002.pdf?name=WBPaper00005490;class=Paper" target="_blank">Herndon 
                            et al., 2002</a>). However, neither condition seems to involve the creation 
                      of extra layers, but just the thickening of certain sublayers. </p>
                    <p class="style33">The cuticle of the <a href="../hermaphrodite/cuticle/Images/cutfig7BCDEleg.htm" target="_blank">buccal cavity and pharynx</a> is qualitatively different 
                      from the body cuticle, lacking layers and forming a series of complex 
                      specializations, including the <a href="../hermaphrodite/cuticle/Images/cutfig7Aleg.htm" target="_blank">flaps</a>, <a href="../hermaphrodite/cuticle/Images/cutfig7BCDEleg.htm" target="_blank">sieve and grinder</a>. It is virtually 
                      separate from the body cuticle except for tenuous connections through 
                      the &#147;bridging cuticle&#148; (Hall, unpublished). </p>
                    <p class="style33">For more detail see <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_cuticle/cuticle.html" target="_blank">Page and Johnstone, 2007</a> and WormAtlas<a href="../hermaphrodite/cuticle/Cutframeset.html">Hermaphrodite Cuticle</a> and<a href="../dauer/cuticle/Cutframeset.html" target="_blank"> Dauer Cuticle</a> sections.</p>
                    <p class="style33">See <a href="bglossary.htm#basallayer" target="_blank">Basal 
                      layer</a><br>
                      See <a href="bglossary.htm#boundaryzone" target="_blank">Boundary 
                        zone</a><br>
                      See <a href="#channel">Channel</a><br>
                      See <a href="#corticallayer">Cortical layer</a><br>
                      See <a href="fglossary.htm#fibrillayer" target="_blank">Fibril layer</a><br>
                      See <a href="mglossary.htm#molt" target="_blank">Molt</a><br>
                      See <a href="mglossary.htm#matrixlayer" target="_blank">Matrix layer</a><br>
                    </p></td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33"><b><a name="cuticlecollagens"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=cuticle&rendertype=figure&id=A15045" target="_blank">Cuticle 
                    collagens</a></b></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style34"></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33">Those collagen proteins that are expressed in the nematode cuticle at 
                    some time during the animal&#146;s development or adult life stages. Genes 
                    encoding collagen proteins are among the larger gene families within the <i>C. elegans</i> genome. Some collagens are not expressed in cuticle, but 
                    instead help to form the basal laminae of various tissues (for more detail see <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_cuticle/cuticle.html#sec4_1" target="_blank">Page and Johnstone, 2007</a>).</span></td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33"><b><a name="cuticleflaps"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/interfacial/Images/interfig1leg.htm" target="_blank">Cuticle 
                    flaps</a></b></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style34"></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33">Three 
                    flaps extend inward from the lining of the buccal cuticle to restrict flow 
                    at the rear of the buccal cavity (<a href="../ver1/Wright_1981/toc.html?name=WBPaper00000531;class=Paper" target="_blank">Wright 
                      and Thomson, 1981</a>). These flaps extend from the pharyngeal muscle cells 
                    m1 and m2. They are made of specialized cuticle and appear very electron 
                    dense in thin section, suggesting a sclerotic hardening to stiffen the flaps 
                    and the entryway to the true pharynx. </span></td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33"><b><a name="cuticlefurrows"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/cuticle/Images/cutfig3leg.htm" target="_blank">Cuticle 
                    furrows</a></b></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style34"></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><p class="style33">Shallow <a href="../hermaphrodite/cuticle/Images/cutfig1leg.htm" target="_blank">circumferentially-oriented indentations</a> in the cuticle. The ridges between furrows are the annuli.</p>
                    <p class="style33"><br>
                      See <a href="aglossary.htm#annuli" target="_blank">Annuli</a></p></td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33"><b><a name="cuticularnubbin"></a>Cuticular 
                    nubbin</b></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33">CN</span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33">See <a href="nglossary.htm#nubbin" target="_blank">Nubbin</a></span></td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33"><b><a name="cyk" id="cyk"></a><a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/phenotype/WBPhenotype:0001078" target="_blank">Cyk</a></b></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33">Cytokinesis abnormal (S) </span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33"><b>CY</b>to<b>K</b>inesis 
                    defect. Term defines abnormality in the division of a cell into two daughter 
                    cells<br>
                    following mitosis.</span></td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33"><b><a name="cytoneme"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/somatic gonad/Images/somaticfig2Aleg.htm" target="_blank">Cytoneme</a></b></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style34"></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33">Thin 
                    tapered processes extended by somatic sheath cell #1 over the surface of 
                    the unsheathed gonad, near the transition zone (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1006/dbio.1999.9356" target="_blank">Hall 
                      et al., 1999</a>; <a href="http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/(SICI)1097-0177(200005)218:1<2::AID-DVDY2>3.0.CO;2-W/abstract" target="_blank">Hubbard 
                        and Greenstein, 2000</a>). </span></td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33"><b><a name="cytoplast" id="cytoplast"></a>Cytoplast</b></span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><span class="style33">Minicell 
                    (S)</span></td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18">&nbsp;</td>
                  <td valign=top class="style18"><p class="style33">A non-viable cell fragment 
                    which has broken off from a parent cell, or also it can be a residual fragment of 
                    a dead or dying cell. Cytoplasts most often contain no nuclear material, 
                    and thus cannot reproduce or maintain themselves (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(81)90427-9" target="_blank">Sulston 
                      and Horvitz, 1981</a>). Other categories of cytoplasts may exist as exudates 
                    or waste products which assemble spontaneously outside of any cell, such 
                    as crystalloids in some species.</p>
                    <p class="style33"><br>
                      See <a href="bglossary.htm#bleb" target="_blank">Bleb</a><br>
