						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><b><a name="egglaying" id="egglaying"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/germ line/jump.html?newLink=mainframe.htm&newAnchor=OocyteMaturationovulationandferilzation4" target="_blank">Egg

							<td valign=top><p>Ovulation 
								(S)<br>
								Oviposition (S) <br>
								</p>
									</p>
							</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>The behavioral program that 
								serves to move fertilized eggs down the uterus, through the vulva, and 
								out of the worm onto the substrate. Egg-laying is controlled by the function 
								of the <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig11Aleg.htm" target="_blank">vulval </a>(<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006917" target="_blank">vm1</a> and <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006918" target="_blank">vm2</a> type) and <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig11Aleg.htm" target="_blank">uterine</a> (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006915" target="_blank">um1</a> and <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006916" target="_blank">um2</a> type) muscles. The uterine muscles contract to squeeze the eggs out of the uterus while 
								the vulval muscles contract to open the vulval orifice. Two classes of <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig15leg.htm" target="_blank">motor neurons synapse onto these muscles</a>; the two <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/HSNframeset.html" target="_blank">HSN</a>'s and the six <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/VCframeset.html" target="_blank">VC</a>'s.<a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/HSNframeset.html" target="_blank"> HSN</a>'s are essential for egg-laying while<a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/VCframeset.html" target="_blank"> VC</a>'s are not, as shown by laser 
								ablation studies.<a href="http://www.wormatlas.org/ver1/neurons.htm/hsn.htm" target="_blank"> </a><a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/HSNframeset.html" target="_blank">HSN</a>'s are serotonergic as well as possibly <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_acetylcholine/acetylcholine.html" target="_blank">cholinergic</a>. 
								All <a href="/neurons.htm/VCn.htm" target="_blank">VC</a>'s are <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_acetylcholine/acetylcholine.html" target="_blank">cholinergic</a> and <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/VCframeset.html" target="_blank">VC4</a> and <a href="../neurons/Individual Neurons/VCframeset.html" target="_blank">VC5</a> are also <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_monoamines/monoamines.html#sec5" target="_blank">serotonergic</a>. <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin" target="_blank">Serotonin</a> and <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levamisole" target="_blank">levamisole</a>, nicotinic Ach agonists, strongly induce egg-laying. <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_monoamines/monoamines.html#sec5" target="_blank">Serotonergic</a> and <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_acetylcholine/acetylcholine.html" target="_blank">cholinergic </a>pathways are suggested to act in parallel for this function 
								(<a href="http://www.wormbook.org/wli/wbg15.1p44/" target="_blank">Weinshenker and Thomas, 1997</a>). </p>
									<p><i>C. elegans</i> modulates its rate 
										of egg laying in response to a variety of environmental conditions; absence 
										of food as well as high osmolarity lead to suppression of egg laying, 
										while the volatile attractant isoamyl alcohol stimulates egg laying in 
										the absence of food (<a href="http://www.citeulike.org/user/tharris/article/3310132" target="_blank">Sawin 
						          and Horvitz, 1991</a>).</p>
								<p><br>
									See <a href="#eggretention">Egg retention</a><br>

										of worms</a></p></td>

							<td valign=top><b><a name="eggplate"></a>Egg 
								plate</b></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A culture plate containing 
								very many developing eggs. This may be produced by the &#147;lay-off&#148; 
								method to produce semi-synchronous eggs, or by choosing a highly populous 
								plate and gently washing off all hatched worms, leaving non-synchronized 
								eggs behind. Synchronization can be achieved among the progeny by a &#147;hatch-off&#148; 
								technique, either by picking individual eggs which have reached the desired 
								age, or by allowing eggs to hatch over a narrow time window, and then 
								collecting these recent hatchees by gentle washing.</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="lglossary.htm#layoff" target="_blank">Lay-off</a><br>
										See <a href="hglossary.htm#hatchoff" target="_blank">Hatch-off</a></p></td>
						</tr>

							<td valign=top><p>A tough protective coating 
								that covers the developing embryo very soon after fertilization of the 
								oocyte. Most or all may be formed by secretion from within the embryonic 
								tissue, but an outer &quot;oolemma&quot; layer is reportedly secreted by the uterine sheath

							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top>A channel

							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>This term can refer to many 
								separate developmental processes, including:</p>
									<p><b>Elongation of the embryo:</b> begins soon after the end of gastrulation, and is caused by circumferential 
										squeezing by the hypodermal cells surrounding the embryo, followed by deposition of a cuticle

							<td valign=top><p>A pigmented granular structure found in pharyngeal muscles  in some nematode species other than <em>C. elegans</em> (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5320(71)90019-0" target="_blank">Burr and Webster, 1971</a>;  Bollerup and Burr, 1979). Such eyespots  vary in color, are different from the background coloration of the rest of the  pharynx, and usually organize into simple cup-shaped structures. Pigmented eyespots occur in other nematode  species in association with sheath cells and a possible lens-like element in  the bodywall of the head (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0014-4894(72)90097-5" target="_blank">Croll et al., 1972</a>; Van de Velde and Coomans, 1988). Although  lacking a detectable eyespot, <em>C. elegans</em> is reported to exhibit a behavioral  response to light (<a href="http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1751-1097.1985.tb03529.x" target="_blank">Burr, 1985</a>; Ken Miller, pers. comm.).</p></td>
