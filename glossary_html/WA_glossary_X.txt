							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="xaratio"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=somaticsexdeterm&rendertype=figure&id=A3232" target="_blank">X/A ratio</a> </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>X:A signal (S) </td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>A measure of the relative  number of sex chromosomes (X) to autosomes (A) within the nucleus, which is  critical in sex determination within that cell. Cells possessing a low ratio  of X/A markers in their nucleus will adopt a male fate, while those with higher  ratios (above a certain critical limit) will adopt female fates (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(84)90077-0" target="_blank">Doniach and Hodgkin, 1984</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_somaticsexdeterm/somaticsexdeterm.html" target="_blank">Zarkower, 2006</a>).</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="sglossary.htm#sexdetermination" target="_blank">Sex determination</a> </p></td>

							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="xchromosom"></a><a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/go_term/GO:0000805" target="_blank">X chromosome</a> </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>The number of copies of this chromosome determines the sex of the animal in <em>C. elegans</em> such that animals with two X chromosomes (XX) become <a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig1leg.htm" target="_blank">hermaphrodites</a> while animals with a single X chromosome (XO) become <a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig5leg.htm" target="_blank">males</a> (for details see <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_hermcellfate/hermcellfate.html" target="_blank">Herman, 2006</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_maledevelopment/maledevelopment.html" target="_blank">Emmons, 2005</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_sexgermline.2/sexgermline.html" target="_blank">Ellis and Schedl, 2007</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_somaticsexdeterm/somaticsexdeterm.html" target="_blank">Zarkower, 2006</a>). As hermaphrodites have two copies and males only have one, hermaphrodites reduce the expression from  this chromosome in a process called dosage compensation (for details see <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_dosagecomp/dosagecomp.html" target="_blank">Meyer, 2005</a>).</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="sglossary.htm#sexdetermination" target="_blank">Sex determination</a></p></td>

							<td valign=top><p>Xenic medium </p></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A culture medium that includes one or more live  microorganisms as part of the food for the cultured species (of nematode).  Nematodes may be successfully raised upon bacteria, fungi, amoebae, dinoflagellates,  or algae, although each nematode species may have a restricted set of foods  which it tolerates (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000255;class=Paper" target="_blank">Nicholas,  1975</a>). A culture medium containing only  one strain of bacterium is termed monoxenic, or dixenic if it contains a mixture  of two bacteria, or a bacterium plus an alga.</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="aglossary.htm#axenicmedium" target="_blank">Axenic medium</a> <br>
										See <a href="dglossary.htm#definedmedium" target="_blank">Defined medium</a></p></td>

							<td valign=top>5-bromo-4-chloro-<br>
								3-indolyl-&beta;-D-galactoside</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A chemical  substrate commonly used to detect lacZ enzyme activity which produces a <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_transcriptionalregulation/transfig1.jpg" target="_blank">blue stain</a> visible by light  microscopy.</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="lglossary.htm#lacz" target="_blank">lacZ </a></p></td>

						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><a name="xxxcell"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/atypical/Atypframeset.html" target="_blank">XXX cell</a> </strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>Two embryonic hypodermal cells (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0004581" target="_blank">XXXL</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0004579" target="_blank">XXXR</a>) that may provide a scaffold for the early organization of ventral bodywall  muscles and the various socket cells in the<a href="../hermaphrodite/introduction/IMAGES/introfig4Aleg.htm" target="_blank"> lateral and ventral lips</a>. In the <a href="../hermaphrodite/atypical/Images/atypfig1leg.htm" target="_blank">developing embryo</a>, their cell bodies soon crawl posteriorly, and  their processes separate the amphid nerve from the mechanosensory nerves in the  late embryo. The XXX processes later  retract, and the XXX cell bodies remain in the<a href="../hermaphrodite/atypical/Images/atypfig2leg.htm" target="_blank"> ventrolateral bodywall</a> near the  cell bodies of the posterior arcade and some socket cells where they perform a neurohumoral function, as  they retain short processes bordering the pseudocoelom and contain some  secretory vesicles. In early larval stages, XXX cells regulate dauer entry by secretion of several steroid signals (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/S1534-5807(01)00085-5" target="_blank">Gerisch et al., 2001</a>; <a href="http://dev.biologists.org/content/129/1/221.long" target="_blank">Jia et al., 2002</a>) and in adults they may also have a role in cholesterol trafficking (<a href="http://www.cell.com/current-biology/retrieve/pii/S0960982200004681" target="_blank">Sym et al., 2000</a>). </p></td>
