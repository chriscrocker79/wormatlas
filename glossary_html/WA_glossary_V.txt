						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="vacuole"></a>Vacuole</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><p>&nbsp;</p></td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A large membrane-bound  organelle within the cell cytoplasm.&nbsp;This term may sometimes be used in reference to distinctive large  endosomes in the intestine, the primary oocyte, or in some sheath cells. </p>
									<p>More properly, it can be used to refer to an  extremely large internal compartment  that develops in the <a href="../hermaphrodite/excretory/Images/excfig5leg.htm" target="_blank">embryonic excretory  canal cell</a> (and perhaps the excretory duct cell) as a precursor to the  excretory sinus and apical excretory canal (<a href="http://www.cell.com/trends/cell-biology//retrieve/pii/S0962892402023644" target="_blank">Buechner, 2002</a>; <a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1126/science.1087667" target="_blank">Berry et al., 2003</a>) that shows<a href="../hermaphrodite/excretory/Images/excfig6leg.htm" target="_blank"> enhanced membrane density</a> compared to any other internal  organelles. </p>
								<p>Vacuoles are often found in cell that are involved in the storage of material such as <a href="../male/somaticgonad/images/MaleReproFIG14B.jpg" target="_blank">secretory cells</a> and <a href="../hermaphrodite/coelomocyte/Images/ccfig5leg.htm" target="_blank">coelomocytes</a>. </p>
								<p>Vacuoles and vacuole-like structures have been noted as features of<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mboc4.figgrp.3249" target="_blank"> apoptotic</a> and <a href="http://www.jneurosci.org/cgi/content/full/17/3/1033/F6" target="_blank">necrotic</a> cell death.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="vagina" id="vagina"></a>Vagina </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><a href="#vulva">Vulva</a> (S) </td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>Alternate (archaic) term  used by some authors for the vulva (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(76)90002-6" target="_blank">Klass et al., 1976</a>; <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2617852/" target="_blank">Riddle, 1978</a>).</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><a name="valve"></a>Valve</font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>A device that regulates the flow of liquids. In <em>C. elegans </em>valve and valve-like structures have been identified, usually connecting two tissues through which material is being shunted. </p>
									<p>The <a href="../hermaphrodite/pharynx/Images/phafig11leg.htm" target="_blank">pharyngeal-intestinal valve</a> links the lumen from the posterior bulb of the pharynx to the lumen of the anterior intestine. The <a href="../hermaphrodite/rectum/Images/rectfig1leg.htm" target="_blank"> intestinal-rectal (rectal) valve</a> occludes the lumen of the posterior intestine with narrow channels that allow digested material to leak to the rectum and then to the anus. It is not clear that this is a true valve since it is unknown whether the size and state of the opening can be altered. The<a href="../hermaphrodite/somatic gonad/Images/somaticfig10leg.htm" target="_blank"> spermatheca-uterine</a> (sp-ut) valve connects the spermatheca to the uterus for the passage of the oocyte and seals the lumen when empty. </p>
								<p>The <a href="../male/somaticgonad/images/MaleReproFIG14A.jpg" target="_blank">anterior end of the vas deferens</a> may act as a kind of valve to regulate sperm release from the seminal vesicle during ejaculation. </p>
								<p>Even the <a href="../hermaphrodite/pharynx/Images/phafig2leg.htm" target="_blank">pharyngeal grinder</a> is considered a valve by some authors (<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/db/misc/paper?name=WBPaper00000255;class=Paper" target="_blank">Nicholas, 1975</a>).</p>
								<p><br>
									See <a href="pglossary.htm#pharyngealintestinalvalve" target="_blank">Pharyngeal-intestinal valve</a> <br>
									See <a href="rglossary.htm#rectalvalve" target="_blank">Rectal valve </a><br>
									See <a href="sglossary.htm#spermathecauterinevalve" target="_blank">Spermatheca uterine valve</a> <br>
									See <a href="#vasdeferens">Vas deferens</a> </p></td>
						</tr>

The middle portion is composed of cuboidal cells, while in the most posterior region the cells are elongated. </p></td>

							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="ventralcleft"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=epidermalmorphogenesis&rendertype=figure&id=A4435" target="_blank">Ventral cleft </a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>Ventral groove (S) </td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>

The cleft opens as  a pore at 100 minutes after fertilization (60 cell stage), widens to form a  cleft from posterior to anterior along the ventral side, and <a href="../hermaphrodite/hypodermis/Images/hypfig3leg.htm?book=wormbook&part=epidermalmorphogenesis&rendertype=figure&id=A4435" target="_blank">closes at 290  minutes</a> (~320 cells).</p>

							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="ventralcord"></a>Ventral cord </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif">Ventral chord (S) </font></td>

							<td valign=top><p>See <a href="#vulvalessphenotype">Vulvaless phenotype</a> </p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top height="111"><p><strong><a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006762" target="_blank">vulA</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006763" target="_blank">vulB1</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006764" target="_blank">vulB2</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006765" target="_blank">vulC</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006766" target="_blank">vulD</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006767" target="_blank">vulE</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006768" target="_blank">vulF</a></strong></p></td>
							<td valign=top height="111">&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top height="111"><p><span class="style26"></span></p></td>
							<td valign=top height="111"><p><a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig9leg.htm" target="_blank">Seven epithelial syncytia (toroids)</a> that <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig10leg.htm" target="_blank">encircle the vulval passageway</a> in  segmental fashion, produced by the fusions of some 22 larval cells, the vulval  equivalence group (<a href="http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/(SICI)1097-0177(200005)218:1%3C30::AID-DVDY4%3E3.0.CO;2-W/abstract" target="_blank">Shemer and Podbilewicz, 2000</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_cellfusion/cellfusion.html" target="_blank">Podbilewicz, 2006</a>). The outermost toroid is called <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig10leg.htm" target="_blank">vulA,  connecting to the vulval lips, and the innermost toroid is called vulF,  connecting to the uterus</a>.</p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="tglossary.htm#toroidalcells" target="_blank">Toroidal cells </a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="vulva"></a><a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/jump.html?newLink=mainframe.htm&newAnchor=Thevulva3" target="_blank">Vulva</a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>Vagina (S) Terminal female gonoduct (S) Gonopore (S) </td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>The passageway that connects  the hermaphrodite&rsquo;s reproductive tract to the exterior, opening at the midway  point along the body on its ventral surface. The vulva is formed from <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig9leg.htm" target="_blank">a stack of 7 toroids</a> which upon<a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig10leg.htm" target="_blank"> morphogenesis</a> undergo cell fusion to create an <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig5ABleg.htm" target="_blank">open lumen</a>. During <a href="../movies/Mating.mov" target="_blank">male mating</a>, sperm are inserted into the hermaphrodite through  the vulval passageway, whereas during egg-laying, fertilized oocytes are  emitted onto the substrate from the vulva through the <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig6ABleg.htm" target="_blank">slit</a>. </p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top height="111"><p><strong><font face="Arial, 
Helvetica, sans-serif"><a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig2leg.htm" target="_blank">Vulval epithelium </a></font></strong></p></td>
							<td valign=top height="111"><p>&nbsp;</p></td>
							<td valign=top height="111"><p class="style26">&nbsp;</p>
									<p class="style26"><br>
								</p></td>
							<td valign=top height="111"><p>A narrow epithelial tube  that<a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig5ABleg.htm" target="_blank"> lines the opening of the reproductive tract</a> on the ventral surface of the  hermaphrodite, covered by a set of ring-shaped syncytial cells that cover  sequential segments of the tube. The epithelial  cells lie only one cell deep, and form a cuticle lined passageway to the  uterus. <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig12leg.htm" target="_blank">Two pairs of vulval muscles </a>are  anchored to these epithelial cells in order to open the vulval lips and the  passageway during egg-laying.</p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="vulvalequivalencegroup"></a>Vulval equivalence group </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p><a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig7leg.htm" target="_blank">A set of 22 cells</a>, normally  formed by the daughters of the <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006893" target="_blank">P5.p</a>,<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/c_elegans/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006894" target="_blank"> P6.p</a> and <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/c_elegans/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006895" target="_blank">P7.p</a> blast cells, which are fated  to organize among themselves to create the mature vulval epithelium and are induced to  adopt this set of cell fates by<a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig7leg.htm" target="_blank"> a signal produced by the anchor cell</a> (<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(80)90353-X" target="_blank">Sulston and White, 1980</a>; <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_evoldevnematode/evoldevnematode.html" target="_blank">Sommer, 2005</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_vulvamorph/vulvamorph.html" target="_blank">Gupta et al., 2012</a>). The pattern of cell divisions among the blast  cells is controlled separately from the cooperation that occurs later among the daughter  cells during the L4 stage to produce the vulva (<a href="http://www.genetics.org/content/96/2/435.long" target="_blank">Horvitz and Sulston, 1980</a>). If some of these cells are removed by  ablation 10-12 hrs before hatching, additional cells can be recruited from the  daughters of neighboring blast cells, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006891" target="_blank">P3.p</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006892" target="_blank">P4.p</a> or <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/c_elegans/anatomy_term/WBbt:0006896" target="_blank">P8.p</a> in order to complete  the set of 22 cells and thus produce a normal vulva.</p>
								  <p><br>
										See <a href="eglossary.htm#equivalencegroup" target="_blank">Equivalence group </a><br>
										See <a href="tglossary.htm#toroidalcells" target="_blank">Toroidal cells </a> </p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="vulvainduction"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK19658/figure/vulvamorph_figure1/?report=objectonly" target="_blank">Vulval induction</a></font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><span class="style26"></span></td>
							<td valign=top><p>An <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK19658/figure/vulvamorph_figure1/?report=objectonly" target="_blank">intercellular  signaling event</a> by which members of the vulval equivalence group are provoked  to begin the processes of differentiation and <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig7leg.htm" target="_blank">cell division</a> that lead to  the creation of the <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig6ABleg.htm" target="_blank">vulva</a>.&nbsp;This process can<a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=evolutionofdev&rendertype=figure&id=A6901" target="_blank"> differ widely between nematode species</a>, such that <a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=wormbook&part=evolutionofdev&rendertype=figure&id=A6903" target="_blank">one  or several inductive signals</a> may be released by one or more cell types lying  close to the position of the presumptive vulva, and can occur as a one time  event or as a prolonged series of signals (<a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_evoldevnematode/evoldevnematode.html" target="_blank">Sommer, 2005</a>). In <em>C. elegans,</em><a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig7leg.htm" target="_blank"> the anchor cell provides the inductive signal </a>that triggers this <a href="../hermaphrodite/egglaying apparatus/Images/eggfig9leg.htm" target="_blank">patterning event</a>. See <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_vulvaldev/vulvaldev.html" target="_blank">Sternberg, 2005</a> and <a href="http://www.wormbook.org/chapters/www_vulvamorph/vulvamorph.html" target="_blank">Gupta et al., 2012</a>.</p>
								  <p><br>
										See <a href="aglossary.htm#anchorcell" target="_blank">Anchor cell</a></p></td>
						</tr>
						<tr class="style18">
							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="vulvalessphenotype"></a><a href="http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bookshelf/br.fcgi?book=ce2&part=A644&rendertype=figure&id=A647" target="_blank">Vulvaless phenotype</a> </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/all/phenotype/WBPhenotype:0000698" target="_blank">vul </a></td>
							<td valign=top>&nbsp;</td>
							<td valign=top><p>A developmental defect found  in certain classes of mutants (including<a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/c_elegans/gene/WBGene00002991" target="_blank"> lin-2</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/c_elegans/gene/WBGene00002992" target="_blank">lin-3</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/c_elegans/gene/WBGene00002993" target="_blank">lin-4</a>, <a href="http://www.wormbase.org/species/c_elegans/gene/WBGene00002996" target="_blank">lin-7</a>) in which the cell divisions leading to vulva development are abnormal, and the vulval  cells are not produced (<a href="http://www.genetics.org/content/96/2/435.long" target="_blank">Horvitz and Sulston, 1980</a>; <a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(81)90427-9" target="_blank">Sulston and Horvitz, 1981</a>). Without a passageway for the fertilized embryos, the embryos hatch inside the hermaphrodite, resulting in a bag of worms. </p>
									<p><br>
										See <a href="bglossary.htm#bagofworms" target="_blank">Bag of worms </a><br>
										See <a href="mglossary.htm#multivulvaphenotype" target="_blank">Multivulva phenotype</a></p></td>
						</tr>

							<td valign=top><strong><font face="Arial, Helvetica, 
sans-serif"><a name="vulvallocation"></a><a href="../male/introduction/images/MaleIntroFIG3A.jpg" target="_blank">Vulval location</a> </font></strong></td>
							<td valign=top><p>Vulval searching (S) <font face="Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"></font></p></td>
